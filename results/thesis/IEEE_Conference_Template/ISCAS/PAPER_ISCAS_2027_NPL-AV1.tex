\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
In the AOMedia Video 1 (AV1) codec, intra-frame coding selects the block
partitioning through a recursive rate-distortion search. At its fast speed
presets, the reference encoder prunes this search with a convolutional neural
network. This paper presents the Neural Pruner Ladder for AV1 (NPL-AV1), a set
of shallow neural networks that replace the native network at the same speed
preset, keeping its other speed features unchanged. A pre-search pruner removes
partition types before any rate-distortion evaluation. A post-NONE pruner
decides whether the search continues after the unpartitioned node is measured,
a decision that no neural model of the native encoder takes in All-Intra coding.
An extended pruner decides whether the extended partition types are evaluated.
Over the eight UHD 4K sequences of the AOM Common Test Conditions, the post-NONE
pruner achieves time savings of 30.34\% with a Bjontegaard delta bitrate
(BD-BR) of 0.41\% at preset 1. Its BD-BR does not differ significantly from
that of the native network at presets 1 and 2, is significantly lower at high
quality, and is higher at preset 3. Across presets 1 to 3, the pre-search
pruner gives time savings from 40.20\% to 77.30\%. The extended pruner alone
adds up to 0.87 percentage points, and the two combined reach 50.99\% with a
BD-BR of 1.07\% at preset 2. Ten of the thirteen nondominated operating points
belong to NPL-AV1.
\end{abstract}

Video coding, AV1, computational effort reduction, intra-frame block

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec_intro}

The number of Internet users watching online video every month keeps growing
\cite{statista}. The AOMedia Video 1 (AV1) was released in 2018 by the Alliance
for Open Media (AOMedia) \cite{av1site}, alongside the High Efficiency Video
Coding (HEVC) \cite{hevc} and the Versatile Video Coding (VVC) \cite{vvc} of
the ITU-T and ISO/IEC international bodies. Each step of its hybrid coding flow introduces new techniques
\cite{han2021,chen2018}. Its coding efficiency
is superior to that of HEVC under certain conditions \cite{grois2016}, at the
price of encoding times about twice as long \cite{layek2017}.

The processing of the partition tree is one of the large computational costs of
the AV1 reference encoder \cite{bender2023}. Each superblock is divided by a
recursive search that evaluates up to ten partition types per node through
rate-distortion optimization (RDO) \cite{sullivan1998}. The reference software
libaom \cite{libaom} performs this search in full at its highest-quality speed
preset, \texttt{cpu-used=0}. From \texttt{cpu-used=1} on, its All-Intra
configuration prunes this search with a convolutional neural network (CNN), the
\texttt{intra\_cnn\_based\_part\_prune} speed feature \cite{libaom}. This CNN
reads each 64$\times$64 block once and, before any RDO, forces or forbids the
quad-tree split down to 8$\times$8 samples.

Few works act on the computational effort of AV1. None of them acts on the
partition network of its reference encoder. The works \cite{xu2020,xu2021,jeong2019} reduce
the intra prediction mode decision, \cite{kim2019} applies decision trees to
inter prediction, and \cite{correa2020,netto2022} are hardware designs for the
intra predictor rather than for encoder decisions. Neural partition prediction
was integrated into the VP9 encoder through a hierarchical CNN \cite{paul2020}.
For VVC, learning-based partitioning solutions use variance and gradients,
random forests and gradient boosting, texture complexity, or the regression of
rate-distortion (RD) costs
\cite{chen2019,he2021,saldanha2022,song2024,kherchouche2024,kherchouche2025}.
These solutions were designed for the partition structures of those codecs and
evaluated on their encoders, which prevents a direct and fair comparison.

This paper presents the Neural Pruner Ladder for AV1 (NPL-AV1), a set of shallow
multilayer perceptrons (MLPs) that replace the native CNN in the libaom
intra-frame partition search. The main strategy of this solution is to consult
each pruner where the information it reads already exists. A pre-search pruner
acts before any RDO, where the native CNN acts. A post-NONE pruner reads the RD
cost of the unpartitioned node and decides whether the search continues, a
decision that no neural model of the native encoder takes in All-Intra coding.
An extended pruner decides whether the extended partition types of
Section~\ref{sec_search} are evaluated. Every pruner is measured at a fixed
preset with the native CNN disabled, since a preset also changes the transform,
intra mode and loop filter searches: only a replacement at the same preset
measures the partition pruner alone. Over the eight UHD 4K sequences of the AOM
Common Test Conditions (CTC), the post-NONE pruner shows no statistically
significant difference in coding efficiency from the native CNN at presets 1
and 2 and a significantly lower coding efficiency loss at high quality. The
pre-search pruner, alone or with the extended pruner, extends the time savings
of every preset, so each preset gains operating points on both sides of the
native CNN.

\section{The AV1 Intra-Frame Partition Search}
\label{sec_search}

The basic unit of AV1 partitioning is the superblock, of 128$\times$128 or
64$\times$64 samples, which the partition tree divides recursively down to
4$\times$4 samples \cite{han2021}. Each node may take one of ten partition types
\cite{chen2018}. NONE codes the node as a single block, SPLIT divides it into
four square sub-blocks, and HORZ and VERT divide it into two rectangular
sub-blocks. The four AB types join a rectangular half to two square sub-blocks,
and the two 4-way types divide it into four sub-blocks of 4:1 aspect ratio. Together, the AB and
4-way families are the extended partitions.

The libaom encoder evaluates the types of a node in a fixed order, NONE, SPLIT, the
rectangular and then the extended types \cite{libaom}, so what a pruner can read
depends on where it is consulted. Before NONE, only the samples, the quantization
and the decisions of the already coded neighbors exist; after NONE, the rate, the
distortion and the RD cost of the unpartitioned node exist as well, at the price
of having been paid.

The native neural pruners act at both points, but not with the same reach in
All-Intra coding. Before the search, the CNN of Section~\ref{sec_intro} forces or
forbids the split. After NONE, three neural decisions can end the search of the
node or remove candidates from it: a breakout after NONE, an early termination
after SPLIT and the pruning of the rectangular types. A frame-type guard disables
all three in intra-only frames \cite{libaom}. Only two networks stay active after
NONE, and they prune the AB and 4-way types from the RD costs already measured.
Whether the search of a node continues once its unpartitioned cost is
known is, therefore, a decision that no neural model of the native encoder takes
in All-Intra coding.

This work is focused on the intra-frame partition search of AV1 under the
All-Intra (AI) configuration. The intra prediction modes, the transform type and
the loop filters are decided inside each evaluated partition type, not by the
partition search itself, and therefore they are not focused on this work, nor is
the inter-frame partition search.

\section{Proposed NPL-AV1 Solution}
\label{sec_method}

The Neural Pruner Ladder for AV1 (NPL-AV1) inserts MLP pruners at the two points
of the node search identified in Section~\ref{sec_search}, as shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig_npl}. The main strategy of this solution is to consult each pruner
where the information it reads already exists. At each preset, the native CNN is
disabled and replaced by the post-NONE pruner or by the pre-search pruner, the
latter alone or with the extended pruner, and every other speed feature of the
preset is kept as it is.

The pre-search pruner is consulted where the native CNN is and acts on the same
structural axis: it may settle the node as NONE and cut the whole subtree below
it, commit the node to SPLIT, or remove the rectangular types and, with them, the
extended ones. The post-NONE and the extended pruners are consulted after NONE has
been evaluated, the point at which the three structural decisions of the native
encoder are disabled in All-Intra coding. The post-NONE pruner decides whether
the search of the node ends there, and the extended pruner whether the AB and
4-way types are worth evaluating, the only decision that the native networks
still take after NONE. Every pruner only removes candidates, so moving between
the operating points of a preset is a matter of choosing which pruner replaces
the CNN, not of retraining it.

The rule behind the ladder is that two pruners add their time savings when they
remove disjoint sets of candidates, and compete for them when they remove the
same ones. The extended pruner removes only AB and 4-way candidates, and only on
the nodes the pre-search pruner did not settle, so its savings are expected to
add to those of the pre-search pruner. Two limits follow, measured in
Section~\ref{sec_results}: the pre-search and the post-NONE pruners both end
nodes at NONE, so they compete, and from preset 3 on libaom adds a pruning of
the extended partitions from the result of SPLIT, so the extended pruner
competes with it.

\subsection{Features, Dataset and Models}
\label{sec_models}

Every pruner reads features whose extraction demands no rate-distortion
evaluation of its own. The pre-search pruner reads thirty-six features in three
sets. Twenty-four block-content features (BC) are statistics of the samples of
the node, of its quadrants and of its contrast with the parent and sibling
sub-blocks, together with the quantization and the position of the node. Eight
causal-neighbor partition context features (NC) read the partition of the coded
blocks above and on the left, and four coding-state and position features (CSP)
are added to them. The
post-NONE and the extended pruners also read the logarithms of the rate, the
distortion and the RD cost of the measured NONE.

The label of every node comes from an extraction with libaom v3.10.0,
instrumented to record the decision of the encoder with these features at
\texttt{cpu-used=0}, since only an exhaustive search gives a faithful label. All
sixteen UHD 4K sequences of UVG \cite{uvg} were encoded over five frames at
\texttt{cq-level} 20, 32, 43 and 55, with ten sequences kept for training, three
for validation and three for testing. The CTC sequences of
Section~\ref{sec_results} do not belong to UVG, which rules out data leakage
between training, testing and evaluation.

Every pruner is a set of three MLPs, for nodes of 16, 32 and 64 samples, whose
two hidden layers have 64 and 32 ReLU units. The pre-search and the post-NONE
pruners give the probabilities of NONE, of SPLIT and of the remaining types, and
the extended pruner gives the probability $p_{\mathrm{ext}}$ that the best type
of the node is an extended one.

\subsection{Decision Rules and Encoder Integration}
\label{sec_policy}

The pre-search pruner reads its probabilities in a fixed order: the node is
settled as NONE, with the whole subtree below it cut, when
$p_{\mathrm{none}} > \tau_{\mathrm{none}}$; otherwise it is committed to SPLIT
when $p_{\mathrm{split}} > \tau_{\mathrm{split}}$; otherwise its rectangular
types are disabled, together with the extended ones, when
$p_{\mathrm{rest}} < \tau_{\mathrm{rest}}$. The post-NONE pruner ends the search
of the node when its probability of NONE, $p_{\mathrm{stop}}$, exceeds
$\tau_{\mathrm{stop}} = 0.95$, and the extended pruner disables the AB and 4-way
types when $p_{\mathrm{ext}} < \theta_{\mathrm{size}}$, with
$\theta_{\mathrm{size}} = 0.0910$ for nodes of 16 samples, 0.1031 for 32 and
0.0144 for 64.

A higher $\tau_{\mathrm{none}}$ or $\tau_{\mathrm{split}}$, or a lower
$\tau_{\mathrm{rest}}$, demands more confidence from the model before a candidate
is removed. Fewer nodes are then pruned and fewer wrong decisions are taken, so
less coding efficiency is lost, at the price of smaller time savings. Loosening
the three thresholds moves the pruner the other way. Two operating points were
selected at opposite sides of this trade-off, set by
$(\tau_{\mathrm{none}}, \tau_{\mathrm{split}}, \tau_{\mathrm{rest}})$: the
efficiency-first one, $(0.95, 0.90, 0.20)$, which is stricter in all three
thresholds, and the time-first one, $(0.60, 0.85, 0.40)$, which is looser in all
three.

All pruners were implemented in C within libaom v3.10.0. With their compile-time
guard disabled, the build gives the bitstream of the reference software byte for
byte. The native CNN is disabled in the same binary, so at each preset the
partition pruner is the only difference from the native configuration. Since
every pruner removes candidates and never forces one, NPL-AV1 is fully compliant
with the AV1 specification and does not affect the bitstream syntax.

\section{Results and Comparisons}
\label{sec_results}

NPL-AV1 was evaluated in the libaom v3.10.0 reference software under the
All-Intra configuration of the AOM CTC \cite{ctc}. The eight sequences of Class
A1 have 3840$\times$2160 samples, 10-bit depth and 4:2:0 chroma subsampling
\cite{xipha1}. Each sequence was encoded over its first 15 frames at
\texttt{cq-level} 20, 32, 43 and 55, with the two-tile setting of the CTC for 4K content, against
libaom at \texttt{cpu-used=0} as anchor. Three deviations from the CTC are
declared. The quantization is set by \texttt{cq-level}, since this version of
libaom has no \texttt{--qp} option, and the time is the wall time of each
encode. The configurations under evaluation run at presets 1, 2 and 3, while
only the anchor keeps \texttt{cpu-used=0}, since the preset is the controlled
variable of this work. The Bjontegaard delta bitrate (BD-BR) \cite{bjontegaard}
uses the luma peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR), and a
positive value is a coding efficiency loss. With $t(s,q)$ and
$t_{\mathrm{anc}}(s,q)$ the encoding times of a configuration and of the anchor
for sequence $s$ at \texttt{cq-level} $q$, and $S$ and $Q$ the sets of sequences
and of \texttt{cq-level} values, the time savings (TS) are
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{TS} = \frac{100}{|S|} \sum_{s \in S} \frac{1}{|Q|} \sum_{q \in Q}
\left( 1 - \frac{t(s,q)}{t_{\mathrm{anc}}(s,q)} \right) \%.
\label{eq_ts}
\end{equation}
Each configuration was encoded once. Five repeated encodes on one sequence give
0.23 percentage points of standard deviation for TS, so TS differences under 0.46
points are not resolved; the BD-BR comes from the bitstream size and the PSNR,
which are exact.

Table~\ref{tab_replace} gives both metrics for the native CNN and for the
NPL-AV1 pruners that replace it at each preset. The post-NONE pruner and the
native CNN are comparable at presets 1 and 2: 0.414\% against 0.449\% of BD-BR
at preset 1 and 0.516\% against 0.536\% at preset 2, for 30.34\% and 40.73\% of
TS against 32.59\% and 42.72\%. The paired t-tests of Table~\ref{tab_ttest},
over the eight sequences, show no significant BD-BR difference at either
preset. The TS of the post-NONE pruner is lower by 2.25 percentage points at
preset 1 ($p = 0.046$) and by 1.99 at preset 2 ($p = 0.055$). At preset 3
the replacement has a BD-BR higher by 0.662 points, in all eight sequences.

The average over the whole grid is the mean of two different regimes. At
\texttt{cq-level} 20 and 32, the post-NONE pruner lowers the BD-BR of the native
CNN by 0.088 points at preset 1 and by 0.086 at preset 2. Both differences are
significant ($p = 0.043$ and $p = 0.015$), in six and seven of the eight
sequences, at a cost of 4.29 and 3.88 percentage points of TS. At \texttt{cq-level} 43 and 55
the two pruners do not differ in BD-BR. This split by quantization regime was
made after the result over the whole grid and is not corrected for multiple
comparisons; its support is the replication of the same sign and magnitude at
two independent presets. This fact partly explains why a shallow model reaches
the coding efficiency of the native network: it reads the measured RD cost of
NONE, which no native network reads before deciding whether the search of the
node continues.

The pre-search pruner gives the other end of each preset. At the
efficiency-first point it lifts the TS of the native CNN by 5.15 to 7.61
percentage points, at a BD-BR 1.4 to 2.0 times higher. The time-first point
reaches 77.30\% of TS at preset 3, a speedup of 4.46 times, at 4.347\% of BD-BR.

The extended pruner confirms the rule of Section~\ref{sec_method}, as
Table~\ref{tab_seq} shows sequence by sequence. Against the efficiency-first
point re-encoded in its own campaign, it lifts the TS by 0.87 percentage points
at preset 1 and by 0.83 at preset 2, in all eight sequences ($p = 0.001$), at
0.032 and 0.039 points of BD-BR. At preset 2, it lifts the TS of that
re-encoded point from 50.16\% to 50.99\%, with 1.069\% of BD-BR. This is 27 and 21 points of TS per point of
BD-BR, against 15.1 and 14.1 when the thresholds of the pre-search pruner are
loosened from the efficiency-first to the time-first point. At preset 3, where
libaom adds its own pruning of the extended partitions, the gain collapses to
$-0.14$ points in all eight sequences. The pre-search and the post-NONE pruners
compete in the same way: their combination at \texttt{cpu-used=0} saves 1.9
points less than the sum of their separate time savings over four sequences.

One more NPL-AV1 point was measured at \texttt{cpu-used=0}, with the pre-search
pruner at $(0.90, 0.90)$, $\tau_{\mathrm{rest}}$ disabled, and the post-NONE
pruner: 0.160\% of BD-BR for 12.61\% of TS. Among the sixteen configurations
measured, the twelve of Table~\ref{tab_replace}, the three with the extended
pruner and the one at \texttt{cpu-used=0}, thirteen are nondominated in BD-BR and TS, ten from NPL-AV1 and three from the native CNN, one
per preset. No NPL-AV1 point dominates the native CNN at the same preset. The
contribution of NPL-AV1 is, therefore, more operating points beside the native
one: at presets 1 and 2 the post-NONE pruner is on the side of coding
efficiency, and at every preset the pre-search pruner is on the side of time
savings.

The cost of the pruners themselves was measured in the encoder over three frames
of two sequences at preset 1. The post-NONE pruner takes 0.26\% to 0.36\% of the
encoding time, the efficiency-first pre-search pruner 0.41\% to 0.48\% and the
extended pruner 0.37\% to 0.42\%, against 0.13\% to 0.19\% of the native CNN.
The time savings come, therefore, from the removed candidates and not from the
inference. No work in the literature replaces the partition network of the AV1
reference encoder, and the solutions of Section~\ref{sec_intro} were designed
for other codecs, so the native CNN is the reference of comparison of this
work.

\section{Conclusions}

This paper presented NPL-AV1, a set of shallow neural pruners that replace the
native CNN of the AV1 intra-frame partition search at the same speed preset, so
that each preset of libaom gains operating points on both sides of the native
CNN. The pre-search pruner acts where the native CNN acted. The post-NONE pruner
decides, from the RD cost of the unpartitioned node, whether the search of the
node ends, a decision that no neural model of the native encoder takes in
All-Intra coding. The extended pruner decides whether the AB and 4-way types are
evaluated. The pruners were implemented in C within libaom v3.10.0 and evaluated
over the eight CTC Class A1 sequences at presets 1, 2 and 3. The post-NONE
pruner reaches 30.34\% of time savings with 0.41\% of BD-BR at preset 1, 2.25
percentage points of time savings below the native CNN. Its BD-BR does not
differ significantly from that of the native CNN at presets 1 and 2, and it is
significantly lower at high quality. The extended pruner adds 0.87 and 0.83
percentage points at presets 1 and 2, and nothing at preset 3, where libaom adds
its own pruning of the same candidates. Combined with it, the pre-search pruner
reaches 50.99\% of time savings with 1.07\% of BD-BR at preset 2. Besides, to the best of the authors'
knowledge, this is the first work that measures a replacement of the native
partition network of AV1 at the same preset.
