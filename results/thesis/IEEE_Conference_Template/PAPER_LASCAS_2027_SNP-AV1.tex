\begin{abstract}
The AOMedia Video 1 (AV1) is an open and royalty-free video codec whose
intra-frame coding relies on a recursive partition search evaluating ten
partition shapes per node. This search is exhaustive by construction and
dominates the intra-frame encoding time at the highest quality configuration, a
burden that grows with 4K content. This paper presents the Stacked Neural Pruners
for AV1 (SNP-AV1), a machine-learning solution for the AV1 intra-frame partition
search applying two neural networks at two distinct points of the search. The
first network reads thirty-six attributes of low-cost causal context
and decides, before any rate-distortion evaluation is paid, whether the node is
committed to the undivided partition, whether the search recurses only on the
square split, or whether the rectangular candidates are discarded. The second
network reads thirty-nine attributes, which add the rate, the distortion and the
rate-distortion cost measured for the undivided candidate, and decides whether
the four AB and the two 4-way shapes are evaluated at all. Experimental results
show that SNP-AV1 achieves an average time savings of 18.74\%, with a BD-BR of
0.59\%, and that a stronger calibration reaches 32.16\% with a BD-BR of 1.42\%.
Compared with the native speed presets, SNP-AV1 does not dominate them, at a cost
of a higher BD-BR, but it delivers operating points inside the region the preset
ladder leaves uncovered. To the best of the authors'
knowledge, this is the first solution in the literature to stack two learned
pruners at two complementary stages of the AV1 partition search.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec_intro}

Video is the dominant workload of the Internet, and the share of users watching
online video every month has kept growing over the last years \cite{statista}.
Delivering this volume at acceptable quality depends on video coding standards,
and the standards defined by the international standardization bodies, such as
the High Efficiency Video Coding (HEVC) \cite{hevc} and the Versatile Video
Coding (VVC) \cite{vvc} produced by the Motion Picture Expert Group (ISO/IEC
MPEG) and by the Video Coding Experts Group (ITU-T VCEG), involve
royalties that burden the companies interested in developing applications with
them.

Several initiatives have been undertaken to develop an open and royalty-free
alternative for video delivery. The Alliance for Open Media (AOMedia) was founded
by a consortium of leading technology companies and developed its encoder
starting from Google's VP9, Cisco's Thor and Mozilla's Daala codecs. The
resulting specification was released in 2018 and named AOMedia Video 1 (AV1)
\cite{av1site}.

AV1 follows the state-of-the-art flow of hybrid video encoders, splitting the
process into the stages of intra-frame prediction, inter-frame prediction,
transforms, quantization, in-loop filters and entropy coding \cite{han2021}.
AV1 introduced new techniques in each one of these steps when compared with
previous encoders \cite{chen2018}, which is what sustains its coding efficiency.

This coding efficiency comes at the price of computational effort. AV1 was shown
to be superior to HEVC in coding efficiency under certain conditions
\cite{grois2016}, but encoding videos of the same bitrate and resolution took
twice as long \cite{layek2017}. The problem is aggravated in 4K content, where
the number of coding units per frame and the depth of the search structures both
grow.

One of the largest individual costs of AV1 intra-frame coding is the block
partitioning decision. Every superblock is submitted to a recursive search that,
at each node of the partition tree, evaluates the rate-distortion (RD) cost
\cite{sullivan1998} of ten candidate partition shapes and keeps the cheapest one,
descending recursively whenever the square split wins. The search is exhaustive
by construction, since the encoder only knows which shape is the best after
actually coding the block under each of them. In the reference software libaom
\cite{libaom} this exhaustive regime is what the highest quality preset,
\texttt{cpu-used=0}, delivers.

There are a few works in the literature targeting the computational effort of
AV1, and none of them attacks this search. The work \cite{gmrf} proposes GM-RF, a
fast decision algorithm that groups the AV1 intra-frame prediction modes and uses
Random Forest models to select which group is evaluated; it is the authors'
previous work, it employs machine learning, and it targets the mode decision
loop, not the partition search, which prevents a direct and fair comparison. The
works \cite{xu2020} and \cite{xu2021} select AV1 coding tools by heuristic
criteria and by user priority, and the work \cite{jeong2019} accelerates the AV1
intra mode decision using the accuracy of an RD model; all three act on the mode
decision and none of them uses learned models over the partition tree. The works
\cite{correa2020} and \cite{netto2022} are hardware-based solutions for the AV1
intra prediction, so their target is the circuit and not the encoder search, and
they are not comparable with a software solution. The work \cite{kim2019} applies
decision trees to AV1, but to inter-prediction, which is a different tool and a
different search. Learning-based partitioning solutions do exist for VVC, such as
the light gradient boosting machine of \cite{saldanha2022}, the random forest of
\cite{he2021} and the variance and gradient heuristic of \cite{chen2019}, and
their concept can all be adapted to work with the AV1 coding tools, but they were
designed for the quadtree with nested multi-type tree of VVC and evaluated on
another encoder, so a direct and fair comparison is not possible. None of these
works explore learned pruning of the AV1 intra-frame partition search itself.

This paper presents SNP-AV1, a machine-learning solution that inserts two neural
pruners at two distinct stages of the AV1 intra-frame partition search. The main
strategy of this solution is to remove candidates progressively as stronger
evidence becomes available: the first stage decides how much of the tree deserves
to be opened at all, using causal context whose extraction cost is negligible,
and the second stage decides whether the most expensive family of candidates is
worth evaluating, using the rate-distortion evidence that the encoder has by then
already measured for the undivided candidate. The two pruners are not two
independent models placed in the encoder; they form a cascade in which the
cheaper and blinder decision runs first and the costlier and better-informed
decision runs on its residue.

Experimental results over the eight 4K sequences of the Common Test Conditions
(CTC) Class A1 show that SNP-AV1 reaches an average time savings of 18.74\% with
a Bj{\o}ntegaard delta bitrate (BD-BR) of 0.586\% at its deployed operating point,
and 32.16\% with a BD-BR of 1.420\% under a stronger calibration.

\section{Block Partitioning in AV1}

The basic unit of the AV1 partitioning is the superblock, which has 128$\times$128
or 64$\times$64 pixels, and the structure that subdivides it is called partition
tree \cite{han2021}. Each node of this tree may be coded as a single block or
subdivided, and the encoder decides node by node which of the available shapes
minimizes the Lagrangian RD cost.

AV1 allows ten partition types at each node \cite{chen2018}, which this work
organizes into four families according to what they cost and to when the encoder
evaluates them.

The first family holds the two structural decisions. PARTITION\_NONE codes the
node as a single undivided block, and PARTITION\_SPLIT divides it into four
square quadrants, instantiating four new nodes with the same set of candidates.

The second family holds the two rectangular shapes, PARTITION\_HORZ and
PARTITION\_VERT, which cut the node into two halves along the horizontal or the
vertical direction.

The third family holds the four AB shapes, HORZ\_A, HORZ\_B, VERT\_A and VERT\_B,
which are asymmetric partitions combining one rectangular half with two square
quadrants on the other half.

The fourth family holds the two 4-way shapes, HORZ\_4 and VERT\_4, which cut the
node into four parallel strips of 4:1 aspect ratio.

The AB and the 4-way families are referred to jointly as the extended partitions
throughout this paper, because they share the property that motivates the second
stage of the proposed solution: they are the last candidates the encoder
evaluates, they are numerous, and each one of them is individually cheap.

The evaluation order inside a node is fixed and it is what defines which
information has already been paid at each point of the flow. The encoder first
evaluates PARTITION\_NONE, then the square split, then the two rectangular
shapes, and finally the four AB and the two 4-way shapes. A pruner that acts
before the first evaluation is blind to any rate-distortion number, and a pruner
that acts after PARTITION\_NONE has the measured rate, distortion and RD cost of
the undivided candidate, at the price of never being able to save the cost of
producing them.

The distribution of the search cost among these families was measured before any
model was trained, using the native partition timing instrumentation of libaom
over 875{,}317 decision nodes of the three reserved test sequences of the
UVG-based dataset described in Section \ref{sec_data}, in intra-frame coding at
\texttt{cpu-used=0}. The local work of a node is defined as the sum of its
non-recursive candidates, so the square split column is excluded because its
timer encloses the recursion and would count the same work several times.
Table \ref{tab_cost} presents the result, whose denominator is therefore the nine
non-recursive candidates and not the ten shapes of the partition tree. It is
important to highlight that these timings were taken on a generic build without
single instruction multiple data optimizations and at a single quantization
point, so they characterize the relative weight of each family and not an
absolute cost.

The main conclusion when observing Table \ref{tab_cost} is that the cost is
distributed over three blocks of comparable size, and that the extended
partitions are one of them. The six extended shapes consume 34.3\% of the local
search time, ranging from 28.9\% to 41.3\% among the sequences, which is
comparable with the 30.1\% of PARTITION\_NONE and with the 35.6\% of the two
rectangular shapes. It is important to emphasize that no single extended shape
exceeds 7.22\% of the local time, which explains why this cost went unnoticed in
analyses that examined candidates in isolation.

This cost also concentrates in the large blocks, and this is what ties the second
stage of the proposed solution to 4K content. The extended shapes do not apply to
8$\times$8 nodes, where their cost is null; they represent 8.7\% of the local time
in 16$\times$16 nodes, 50.0\% in 32$\times$32 nodes, 51.0\% in 64$\times$64 nodes
and 35.3\% in 128$\times$128 nodes. The large nodes are therefore the ones where
this family is exercised, and they are the ones a 4K frame produces in abundance,
which may be attributed to a substantial part of such a frame being smooth at the
scale of a 64-pixel block. No measurement at lower resolutions was performed in
this work, so this reading is the design rationale of the second stage and not a
measured comparison across resolutions.

This work is focused on the recursive block partitioning decision of the AV1
intra-frame coding. The remaining decisions taken inside a coding block are not
included in the partition search loop attacked here, such as the directional and
non-directional intra prediction modes, the recursive-based filtering, the
chroma-from-luma prediction, the intra block copy, the palette mode, the
transform type search and the in-loop filters, and therefore they are not focused
on this work. The inter-frame partition search is also outside the scope, since
the solution is trained and evaluated exclusively under the All-Intra (AI)
encoder configuration.

\section{Proposed SNP-AV1 Solution}
\label{sec_method}

The Stacked Neural Pruners for AV1 (SNP-AV1) is a fast decision algorithm for the
AV1 intra-frame partition search composed of two neural pruners inserted at two
distinct stages of the search of a node. The main strategy of this solution is to
remove search candidates progressively as stronger evidence becomes available,
so that the decision with the largest reach is taken with the cheapest
information and the decision with the richest information is taken over what the
first one left behind.

\subsection{Rationale for a Cascade}

The two stages are not two levels of sophistication of the same idea; they occupy
different positions in a plane defined by two independent dimensions, which are
when the pruner acts, and therefore which information has already been paid, and
what the pruner removes from the search.

The first stage acts before any evaluation, so its reach is maximal: a correct
commitment removes the whole subtree, including the evaluation of PARTITION\_NONE
itself, which is the only cost no later pruner can ever save. In exchange, it
decides without having observed a single rate-distortion number. The second stage
acts after PARTITION\_NONE has been evaluated, so its information is
substantially richer, since the encoder has already measured what it costs to
code the block as a single unit. Its reach, on the other hand, is structurally
limited, because the cost of PARTITION\_NONE is already paid and is never
recovered.

This is what makes the two stages complementary rather than competing. Two
pruners add up to the extent that the sets of candidates they remove are
disjoint, and the second stage targets a family that the first one never aims at,
which consumes 34.3\% of the local search time as reported in
Table \ref{tab_cost}.

\subsection{Attribute Design}

The attribute vector of the first stage has thirty-six entries, organized in
three blocks whose criterion for inclusion was the cost of obtaining each one at
the exact point of the flow where the pruner is consulted.

The first block holds twenty-four luma descriptors of the node and of its
hierarchical context: overall and per-quadrant variance, dispersion and
heterogeneity among quadrants, horizontal and vertical gradient energy and their
orientation, row-sum and column-sum profiles, strongest edge, strong-edge density
and DC level, together with the texture of the containing 2n$\times$2n block, the
contrast against that parent and against the three sibling quadrants, the
normalized quantization index and the position of the node inside the 64-pixel
unit. This block requires no new instrumentation, since the source pixels of the
node, of its parent and of its siblings are already in the source frame, but it
does require computation that is linear in the area of the node.

The second block holds eight attributes of causal partitioning neighborhood:
availability of the above and left neighbors, their decided widths and heights in
log2 units, the relative granularity of the neighborhood and its anisotropy.
The third block holds four attributes of quantization and position: the effective
dequantization step of the luma DC coefficient, the normalized row and column
position in the frame, and the depth of the node.

The eight attributes of the second block and the four of the third are a free
read of state that already resides in the encoder memory at the moment of the
decision, since the sizes of the neighboring partitions, the quantization step,
the position and the node dimension are all values that the native search already
maintains and already consults. All selected attributes are natively available
during encoding, ensuring zero computational overhead for attribute extraction
beyond the arithmetic itself.

The attribute vector of the second stage has thirty-nine entries: the same
thirty-six of the first stage, plus three attributes that only exist after the
undivided candidate has been evaluated, which are the logarithm of the rate, of
the distortion and of the RD cost measured for PARTITION\_NONE. These three
attributes are the reason the second stage is better informed and, at the same
time, the reason it can never act earlier.

\subsection{Dataset Construction}
\label{sec_data}

The reference software libaom v3.10.0 was instrumented under a compile-time guard
to record, at every node of the recursive intra-frame partition search, the
ground-truth partition decision taken by the encoder itself, together with the
luma samples of the node, the causal partitioning context, the quantization
state, the position and the rate-distortion result of PARTITION\_NONE. The guard
is necessary because a binary that writes to disk at every node cannot be the
same binary that measures wall-clock acceleration; with the guard disabled, the
production build is verified byte-identical to the untouched reference tree.

The dataset was built from the sixteen 4K sequences of the UVG dataset
\cite{uvg}, at 3840$\times$2160, 8-bit depth and 4:2:0 chroma subsampling. Each
sequence was encoded at four constant quality (CQ) values, 20, 32, 43 and 55,
over five frames sampled uniformly along the whole clip. The temporally spread
sampling is a methodological decision and not a convenience, since in AI
coding consecutive frames are nearly identical and a temporally dense dataset
would be poorly representative; diversity therefore comes from four combined
axes, which are spatial, content, quantization and temporal.

The extraction was executed entirely at \texttt{cpu-used=0}. This decision is the
most consequential of the whole procedure, because the ground-truth label is only
faithful if it comes from a search that actually evaluated every candidate shape.
Any faster preset already contains heuristic pruning and early termination and
would contaminate the labels with the decisions of another heuristic instead of
the RD-optimal decision that is to be learned. The contamination was verified
rather than assumed: an extraction at \texttt{cpu-used=6} produced zero counts for
the six extended classes, which are exactly the classes the second stage must
predict, whereas at \texttt{cpu-used=0} all ten classes appear. The price of this
decision was 32.3 hours of continuous encoding for the sixty-four extraction
tasks.

The resulting dataset contains 26.98 million samples, one per visited node, of
which 10.07 million are decision nodes of 16, 32 and 64 pixels. The remaining
16.91 million are 8$\times$8 leaves whose label is constant and which are therefore
left out of the modeling, although they remain in the tree accounting, since
pruning a 16$\times$16 node saves exactly its four children. The class imbalance
is strong and depends on the node size: in 64$\times$64 nodes the split label
accounts for 64.12\% of the samples and the undivided label for 28.84\%; in
32$\times$32 nodes the undivided label rises to 47.72\% and the split label falls
to 28.86\%; and in 16$\times$16 nodes the undivided label dominates with 73.46\%
against only 4.40\% of split.

The dataset was partitioned by sequence into ten training sequences, three
validation sequences, which are HoneyBee, FlowerPan and Lips, and three reserved
test sequences, which are Jockey, RaceNight and RiverBank, and the partition was
frozen before any measurement. Furthermore, the eight sequences used for the
final evaluation belong to the CTC Class A1 set and none of them appears in the
UVG corpus. Consequently, no video sequence was reused across the training,
testing, or evaluation datasets, thus avoiding data leakage and overfitting.

\subsection{Network Architecture and Training}

Both stages use multilayer perceptrons, one per node size, trained independently
for the 16$\times$16, 32$\times$32 and 64$\times$64 levels. The 8$\times$8 level is
not modeled, since it is terminal and the native pruning already handles it.

The topology is common to all variants and deliberately shallow: two hidden
layers of 64 and 32 units with rectified activation, followed by a linear output
layer. The first stage takes thirty-six inputs and emits three classes, whose
softmax is interpreted as the probability of the node being undivided, of it
being split, and of it taking any of the remaining shapes. The second stage takes
thirty-nine inputs and emits two classes, corresponding to evaluating or skipping
the extended partitions. This gives 4{,}547 parameters per level in the first
stage and 4{,}706 per level in the second, for a total of 27{,}759 parameters
across the six deployed networks. The weight layout matches exactly what the
native inference routine of the encoder expects, which dispenses with any new
inference code in C.

The networks were trained in PyTorch \cite{pytorch} with the AdamW optimizer
\cite{adamw}, a learning rate of $1 \times 10^{-3}$, a weight decay of
$1 \times 10^{-4}$, batches of 4096 samples and a fixed budget of 30 epochs
without early stopping. The loss is the hard-label cross entropy without class
weighting, and this is a measured design decision rather than an assumed one:
training the same topology with inverse-frequency class weights improves
classification, raising the split recall in 16$\times$16 nodes from 0.022 to
0.361 and the macro-F1 from 0.524 to 0.556, but it degrades the expected
calibration error of the undivided class from 0.021 to 0.174, which is eight
times worse. Since the policy is thresholded over the probability, it is
calibration, and not recall, that determines what the encoder pays.

The attributes are standardized during training and the standardization is folded
into the first linear layer at export time. Consequently, the encoder feeds the
networks with raw attributes and the parity between the training and the encoder
implementations becomes directly verifiable. This parity was verified over 192
random vectors, with a maximum difference of $1.35 \times 10^{-7}$ between the
training output and the deployed arrangement, which is exact up to floating-point
error.

\subsection{First Stage: Pre-Search Pruning}

The first-stage pruner is consulted at the beginning of the node, before any
candidate is evaluated, and applies three actions in cascade, controlled by three
thresholds.

If the probability of the undivided class exceeds $\tau_{\mathrm{none}}$, all
splits are disabled and the node is decided as PARTITION\_NONE, which cuts the
whole subtree without recursion and without evaluating any candidate. It is the
primary lever of time savings. Otherwise, if the probability of the split class
exceeds $\tau_{\mathrm{split}}$, the shape candidates of the node are skipped and
the search recurses only into the four quadrants. Otherwise, if the probability
of the remaining class falls below $\tau_{\mathrm{rest}}$, the search between the
undivided
candidate and the square split is preserved and the rectangular candidates are
disabled, which by dependency also disables the extended shapes. If none of the
three conditions holds, the node proceeds to the full search.

Two operating points of this stage were frozen before the final campaign. The
balanced point uses $\tau_{\mathrm{none}} = 0.95$, $\tau_{\mathrm{split}} = 0.90$
and $\tau_{\mathrm{rest}} = 0.20$, and the aggressive point uses 0.60, 0.85
and 0.40. They are
two settings of the same control and not two models, so moving between them
requires neither retraining nor recompilation.

The interpretation of these thresholds as degrees of confidence was verified
rather than supposed. Over 1{,}816{,}393 decision nodes of the reserved test set,
the expected calibration error of the predicted class is 0.0112, and the
precision at the thresholds is slightly above nominal, reaching 97.2\% for the
undivided class and 98.4\% for the split class at 0.95.

\subsection{Second Stage: Extended-Partition Pruning}

The second-stage pruner is consulted immediately after PARTITION\_NONE has been
evaluated. It computes the thirty-nine attributes, runs the inference and marks
the node to skip the extended partitions when the estimated probability falls
below a threshold. PARTITION\_NONE, the square split and the rectangular shapes
are left untouched.

The viability of this stage was decided outside the encoder, before any
integration cost was paid. The binary label was defined per node as positive when
the optimal partition belongs to the extended set and negative otherwise, which
is exactly the question the pruner must answer, and it was evaluated over
792{,}840 decision nodes of the six sequences outside the training split. The
area under the receiver operating characteristic curve is 0.902 in the aggregate,
with 0.919 at the 16-pixel level, 0.829 at the 32-pixel level and 0.865 at the
64-pixel level, over a positive base of only 10.2\%. In terms of the trade the
pruner faces, the model avoids 69.7\% of the extended searches while losing 10\%
of the true winners, or avoids 50\% of them while losing only 1.1\%.

The thresholds are kept per node size rather than global, and the reason is
experimental. The thresholds that discard the same fraction of true winners
differ widely between levels, and the deployed calibration, which discards
approximately 10\% of the winners, uses 0.0910 at the 16-pixel level, 0.1031 at
the 32-pixel level and only 0.0144 at the 64-pixel level. A single global
threshold of 0.30 improved two sequences and degraded a third, whereas the
per-level calibration recovered the degraded sequence without giving up the gain
on the others. A stronger calibration, discarding approximately 20\% of the
winners, uses 0.1776, 0.1627 and 0.0321, and is reported as an additional
operating point in Section \ref{sec_results}.

\subsection{Encoder Integration and Compliance}

Both pruners were implemented in C inside libaom v3.10.0 and execute natively
within the encoding pipeline, using the inference routine already present in the
reference software. The policy only removes candidates and never forces an
illegal candidate, so the search flow remains valid and SNP-AV1 is fully
compliant with the AV1 specification and does not affect the bitstream syntax, so
the generated bitstream is decodable by any conforming AV1 decoder.

The whole control surface is inert by default, and this inertia was verified
rather than declared. Re-encoding a reference point with the second stage
switched off reproduces the first-stage baseline byte for byte, at 1{,}574{,}775
bytes and a luma peak signal-to-noise ratio (Y-PSNR) of 40.9720 dB against the
same 1{,}574{,}775 bytes and 40.9720 dB of the reference. Consequently, the marginal contribution reported in
Section \ref{sec_results} cannot be attributed to a compilation difference, to an
instrumentation side effect or to a configuration drift.

It is important to highlight the structural limits of the proposed solution. The
first stage is blind to rate-distortion evidence by construction, which caps how
confidently it can commit a node, and the second stage can never recover the cost
of the undivided candidate it depends upon, which caps its reach regardless of
how accurate it becomes. These two limits are what the cascade trades against
each other, and they anticipate the reading of the results.

\section{Results and Comparisons}
\label{sec_results}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

The developed solution was evaluated using the libaom v3.10.0 AV1 reference
software, following the AOM Common Test Conditions (CTC) \cite{ctc} and under the
AI encoder configuration. The evaluation used the eight 4K sequences of
the CTC Class A1 test set, which are BoxingPractice, Crosswalk, FoodMarket2,
Neon1224, NocturneDance, PierSeaSide, Tango and TimeLapse, all at
3840$\times$2160, 10-bit depth and 4:2:0 chroma subsampling, obtained from the
XIPH repository. The first 15 frames of each sequence were encoded, which is what
the CTC specifies for the AI scenario, at four CQ values, 20, 32, 43
and 55.

The anchor is the unmodified libaom v3.10.0 running at \texttt{cpu-used=0}, which
is the regime in which the partition search is exhaustive. Three metrics are
reported. The BD-BR \cite{bjontegaard} measures the cost in coding efficiency over
the Y-PSNR, and a negative BD-BR denotes a coding efficiency gain, so all
values reported here, being positive, are losses. The time savings (TS) express
in percentage points the wall-clock time saved relative to the anchor, computed
per CQ value as $1 - t_{config}/t_{anchor}$, averaged over the four CQ values and
then over the sequences. The speedup is the ratio between the anchor time and the
configuration time. It is important to highlight that the BD-BR values are exact
rather than noisy estimates, since the byte count and the Y-PSNR are
deterministic for a given encoder and a given input; the only component with
run-to-run variance is the wall-clock time, whose measured resolution in this
arrangement is approximately 0.46 percentage points.

All BD-BR and TS results reported in this section were measured on the CTC Class
A1 grid. The statistics of Table \ref{tab_cost} and the offline figures of
Section \ref{sec_method} were measured on the UVG-based dataset and are distribution and
classification statistics, never coding efficiency or time results.

anchor, so the two arms are clean and disjoint.

the preset ladder does not offer. The native ladder jumps from
\texttt{cpu-used=0}, with no time savings at all, straight to \texttt{cpu-used=1}
at roughly 32.6\%, and no intermediate step exists. SNP-AV1 delivers six measured
operating points inside that void, three of them clustered between 17.72\% and
19.81\%, obtained by changing thresholds without retraining and without
recompiling.

\subsection{Marginal Contribution of the Second Stage}

Because the first stage runs first and the second stage only acts on the residue
of nodes it did not commit, the correct measurement of the second stage is
marginal: the same binary, with and without the second pruner, over the same
anchor. Comparing it against the pristine encoder would double-count the time the
first stage already collects.

In this frame, the second stage contributes $+1.02$ percentage points of time
savings at a cost of $+0.018$ percentage points of BD-BR over the balanced base,
which is a price of 0.018 percentage points of BD-BR per percentage point of time
saved. This price is directly comparable with the only alternative mechanism a
user of the deployed solution has, which is loosening the thresholds of the first
stage. The segment between the balanced and the aggressive settings of that knob, both
of them measured on the same CTC grid and interpolated per sequence, costs 0.063
percentage points per percentage point, so the second stage buys time
approximately 3.5 times cheaper than the threshold knob does. The same comparison
was repeated for the four combinations of two first-stage bases and two
second-stage calibrations, and all four beat the threshold knob, with cost ratios
from 1.52 to 3.38 times, the deployed pair being the best of the four.

Observing the per-sequence data of Table \ref{tab_seq}, the second stage
increases the time savings in all eight sequences and, in two of them, it does so
while also reducing the BD-BR. On FoodMarket2 the BD-BR falls from 0.628\% to
0.609\% with $+1.8$ percentage points of time saved, and on Tango it falls from
1.153\% to 1.139\% with $+1.6$ percentage points, which are two cases of strict
Pareto dominance over the first stage alone. Against the threshold curve of the
first stage at matched time savings, interpolated per sequence between the two
measured first-stage points, the second stage is better in six of the eight
sequences, and it loses slightly on Crosswalk and Neon1224, which are
precisely the two sequences where its time gain is nearly null, at 0.4 and 0.1
percentage points. In other words, it loses where it has almost nothing to prune.

Another important observation is that this additivity is a function of the
operating point and not an absolute property of the cascade. Over the aggressive
base, the marginal contribution collapses to $+0.17$ percentage points, which is
below the measured temporal resolution of 0.46 percentage points and must
therefore not be cited as positive. With the undivided threshold loosened to
0.60, the first stage commits the nodes so early that they never reach the second
pruner, and the two stages start disputing the same residue. This fact partly
explains why the deployed pair uses the balanced base: it is the setting in which
the disjunction between the two action sets is maximal.

\subsection{Computational Overhead}

The overhead of the proposed solution was measured inside the encoder against the
wall-clock time of the same encoding, and it is reported rather than omitted. The
complete path of SNP-AV1, with attribute extraction and inference added together,
costs between 0.26\% and 0.32\% of the encoding time, whereas the native learned
pruner of libaom costs between 0.16\% and 0.21\%. Both are below one third of one
percent, so the time savings reported in Table \ref{tab_ctc} come entirely from
the pruning decisions and not from the lightness of each inference. Conversely,
the lightness of the networks is not claimed as an advantage of this proposal,
since it is not what sustains the measured gain.

\subsection{Comparison with Related Works}

A direct and fair comparison with the AV1 works discussed in Section \ref{sec_intro} is not
possible, and the reason must be stated before any number. The works
\cite{gmrf,xu2020,xu2021,jeong2019} target the AV1
intra prediction mode decision and not the partition search, so they attack a
different tool, they employ a different version of libaom, a distinct set of
video sequences at lower resolutions, and a different anchor preset. Therefore,
highlighting this limitation, the results in \cite{gmrf} reported an average time
savings of 50.19\% with a BD-BR of 7.41\% over HD720 and HD1080 sequences. That
solution trades roughly 0.15 percentage points of BD-BR per percentage point of
time saved, against the 0.018 of the second stage proposed here, but the two
figures measure different tools under different anchors and the comparison is
offered as context and not as a ranking.

The learning-based partitioning solutions of \cite{saldanha2022,he2021,chen2019}
operate on the VVC quadtree with nested multi-type tree, which is a different
partition structure evaluated on a different reference encoder, so a direct and
fair comparison is not possible either.

\section{Conclusions}

This paper presented SNP-AV1, a machine-learning solution that stacks two neural
pruners at two distinct stages of the AV1 intra-frame partition search, reaching
an average time savings of 18.74\% with a BD-BR of 0.59\% at its deployed
operating point, and 32.16\% with a BD-BR of 1.42\% under a stronger calibration.

The solution works by removing search candidates progressively as stronger
evidence becomes available. The first pruner reads thirty-six attributes of
low-cost causal context and decides, before any rate-distortion cost is paid,
whether the node is committed to the undivided partition, whether the search
recurses only on the square split, or whether the rectangular candidates are
discarded. The second pruner reads thirty-nine attributes, which add the rate,
the distortion and the RD cost measured for the undivided candidate, and decides
whether the four AB shapes and the two 4-way shapes are evaluated at all. The two
sets of pruned candidates are disjoint at the deployed operating point, which is
why the second stage adds 1.02 percentage points of time savings for 0.018
percentage points of BD-BR over the first one, buying time approximately 3.5
times cheaper than the threshold knob of the first stage.

Both pruners were implemented in C inside the libaom v3.10.0 reference software
and execute natively within the encoding pipeline, and the solution was evaluated
end to end over the eight 4K sequences of the CTC Class A1 set, under the AI
configuration, against the exhaustive \texttt{cpu-used=0} anchor. The
whole overhead of attribute extraction and inference stays below 0.32\% of the
encoding time.

Compared with the native speed presets of libaom, no operating point of SNP-AV1
dominates the preset ladder on both axes: \texttt{cpu-used=1} delivers 32.59\% of
time savings at a BD-BR of 0.449\%, against 18.74\% at 0.586\%. What SNP-AV1
delivers is complexity control at a granularity the ladder does not offer, since
the native encoder jumps from no acceleration at all directly to roughly 32.6\%,
and the proposed solution populates that void with operating points calibrated by
threshold alone, without retraining and without recompiling. Besides, to the best
of the authors' knowledge, this is the first solution in the literature to stack
two learned pruners at two complementary stages of the AV1 intra-frame partition
search.
