\begin{abstract}
The intra-frame coding of the AOMedia Video 1 (AV1) codec relies on a recursive
block partition search that evaluates up to ten partition types at each node of
the partition tree. At the highest-quality configuration this search is exhaustive by
construction and dominates the intra-frame encoding time. This paper presents the
Stacked Neural Pruners for AV1 (SNP-AV1), a fast decision algorithm applying two
neural networks at two distinct stages of a single node of that search. The first
reads thirty-six pre-search features, whose extraction cost is negligible, and
decides before any rate-distortion evaluation how much of the subtree deserves to
be opened. The second adds the measured rate-distortion cost of the unpartitioned
node and decides whether the AB and 4-way partition types, one third of the local
search time and a family no earlier predictor targeted, are evaluated at all. The two are stacked because the candidates they remove are disjoint.
Experimental results show that SNP-AV1 achieves an average time savings of
18.74\% with a Bjontegaard delta bitrate (BD-BR) of 0.59\%, reaching 32.16\% and
1.42\% under a stronger calibration, and the second pruner buys time about 3.4
times cheaper than loosening the thresholds of the first.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Video coding, AV1, computational effort reduction, intra-frame block

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec_intro}

Video is the dominant workload of the Internet, and the share of users watching
online video every month has kept growing over the last years \cite{statista}.
Alongside the standards of the international bodies, such as the High Efficiency
Video Coding (HEVC) \cite{hevc} and the Versatile Video Coding (VVC) \cite{vvc},
from the Motion Picture Expert Group (ISO/IEC MPEG) and the Video Coding Experts
Group (ITU-T VCEG), the Alliance for Open Media (AOMedia) maintains the AOMedia
Video 1 (AV1) \cite{av1site}. AV1 follows the hybrid coding flow and introduced
new techniques in each of its steps \cite{han2021,chen2018}. This coding
efficiency comes at the price of computational effort: AV1 was shown to be
superior to HEVC under certain conditions \cite{grois2016}, but encoding took
twice as long \cite{layek2017}, a problem aggravated during UHD 4K content
processing.

One of the largest individual costs of AV1 intra-frame coding is the block
partitioning decision \cite{bender2023}. Every superblock is submitted to a
recursive search that,
at each node of the partition tree, evaluates up to ten partition types by
rate-distortion optimization (RDO) \cite{sullivan1998} and keeps the cheapest one,
descending recursively whenever the split wins. The search is exhaustive by
construction, since the best partition type is only known after the node is
evaluated under every candidate, and the reference software libaom \cite{libaom}
performs it in full at the highest-quality setting, \texttt{cpu-used=0}.

There are a few works in the literature targeting the computational effort of
AV1, and none of them deals with this search. The works \cite{xu2020,xu2021,%
jeong2019} act on the intra prediction mode decision loop, \cite{correa2020,%
netto2022} are hardware solutions for the AV1 intra prediction, whose target is
the circuit and not the encoder search, and \cite{kim2019} uses decision trees on
the inter-prediction modes. Learning-based partitioning solutions do exist for
VVC, over variance, random forests, gradient boosting, texture complexity and
the regression of RD costs \cite{saldanha2022,he2021,chen2019,song2024,%
kherchouche2024,kherchouche2025}, and can be adapted to the AV1 coding tools,
but they were designed for the quad-tree with nested multi-type tree and
evaluated on another encoder. None allows a direct and fair comparison, and none
acts on the recursive partition search of AV1.

This paper presents the Stacked Neural Pruners for AV1 (SNP-AV1), a fast decision
algorithm confined to the intra-frame partition search, which leaves every other
encoding stage untouched. The main strategy of this solution is to remove
candidates progressively, as stronger evidence becomes available: a first network
decides how much of the partition tree deserves to be opened, from pre-search
information whose extraction cost is negligible, and a second decides whether the
most expensive family of candidates is worth evaluating, from the
rate-distortion evidence the encoder has measured by then. The two are stacked
because the sets of candidates they remove are disjoint, the condition under
which two pruners accumulate instead of competing for the same time. Over the
eight UHD 4K video sequences of the Common Test Conditions (CTC) Class A1,
SNP-AV1 reaches an average time savings of 18.74\% with a Bj{\o}ntegaard delta
bitrate (BD-BR) of 0.586\%.

\section{The AV1 Intra Partition Search}
\label{sec_shapes}

The basic unit of the AV1 partitioning is the superblock, of 128$\times$128 or
64$\times$64 samples, and the structure that subdivides it is the partition tree
\cite{han2021}. AV1 supports up to ten partition types at a node \cite{chen2018},
grouped here into four families: the two structural decisions,
\texttt{PARTITION\_NONE}, hereafter NONE, which codes the node unpartitioned, and
\texttt{PARTITION\_SPLIT}, the quad-tree split into four square sub-blocks; the two
rectangular types, \texttt{PARTITION\_HORZ} and \texttt{PARTITION\_VERT}; the four
asymmetric AB types, each combining one rectangular half with two square
sub-blocks; and the two 4-way types, of 4:1 aspect ratio. The AB and the 4-way
families are jointly called the extended partitions.

The evaluation order inside a node is fixed and defines which information has
already been paid at each point of the flow: NONE, the split, the rectangular
types and finally the extended partitions. A predictor consulted at the
pre-search point is blind to any rate-distortion information; one consulted after
NONE reads the rate, the distortion and the RD cost of the unpartitioned node, at
the price of never saving the cost of producing them.

The cost of the search was measured with the native partition timing
instrumentation of libaom over 875{,}317 decision nodes of the UHD 4K sequences of
Section~\ref{sec_data}, at \texttt{cpu-used=0}, taking as local work the sum of
the nine non-recursive candidates of a node. It splits into three blocks of
comparable size: NONE takes 30.1\% of that time, the two rectangular types 35.6\%
and the six extended ones 34.3\%. No single extended type exceeds 7.22\%, which is
why the weight of the family went unnoticed, and the cost concentrates in the
large nodes, reaching 50.0\% at 32$\times$32 and 51.0\% at 64$\times$64 samples,
the nodes a UHD 4K frame produces in abundance.

This work is focused on the recursive block partitioning decision of the AV1
intra-frame coding. The other decisions taken inside a coding block, such as the
intra prediction modes, the transform type search and the in-loop filtering, lie
outside the partition search loop addressed here, as does the inter-frame
partition search, since the solution is trained and evaluated exclusively under
the All-Intra (AI) encoder configuration.

\section{Proposed SNP-AV1 Solution}
\label{sec_method}

The Stacked Neural Pruners for AV1 (SNP-AV1) is a fast decision algorithm that
inserts two neural pruners at two distinct stages of the search of a node, as
shown in Fig.~\ref{fig_snp}. The main strategy of this solution is to consult
each pruner at the earliest point of the flow at which the information it reads
has already been paid for.

The rule behind the pairing is that two pruners in the same search compete for
the same saved time whenever they remove the same candidates, however different
the information each reads, and accumulate whenever the sets they remove are
disjoint. The first asks, at the pre-search point, whether the node can be
settled at once on the structural axis of NONE against the split, so a commitment
removes every competing candidate of the node and the whole subtree below it; the
second asks only, and only on the nodes that survived, whether the extended
partitions, the block of cost isolated in Section~\ref{sec_shapes}, are worth
evaluating. One limit follows, measured in Section~\ref{sec_results}: a node the
first stage has committed never reaches the second, so the disjunction is a
property of the operating point, and not of the pruners.

\subsection{Pre-Search Feature Sets}

Both stages read the same pool of pre-search features, organized into three named
sets. The block-content features (BC) are twenty-four compact descriptors of the
node and of its hierarchical context: variance and gradient statistics of the
block and of its quadrants, edge strength and density, row and column profiles,
the contrast against the parent and the sibling sub-blocks, the normalized
quantization index and the position inside the 64$\times$64 unit. The
causal-neighbor partition context (NC) adds eight features from the already coded
blocks above and to the left: their availability, their decided widths and
heights in log2 units, and the granularity and the anisotropy of the surrounding
partition structure. The coding-state and position features (CSP) add four: the
effective DC dequantization step, the normalized position in the frame and the
node depth. The first stage reads BC+NC+CSP, thirty-six features in all; the
second reads the same thirty-six plus the logarithm of the rate, of the
distortion and of the RD cost measured for NONE, for thirty-nine.

The criterion for inclusion was the cost of each feature where the pruner is
consulted: NC and CSP are a free read of state the search already maintains, and
BC is arithmetic linear in the area of the node, so no feature demands a
rate-distortion evaluation of its own. Every luma sample is brought to the
eight-bit scale first, so models trained on eight-bit material are deployed
unchanged on the ten-bit evaluation sequences.

\subsection{Reference Encoding and Dataset}
\label{sec_data}

The reference software libaom v3.10.0 was instrumented, under a compile-time
guard so that the writing binary is never the one that measures acceleration, to
record at every node of the intra-frame partition search the decision taken by
the encoder together with the luma samples, the causal context, the quantization
state, the position and the rate-distortion result of NONE. The dataset was built
from the sixteen UHD 4K sequences of the UVG dataset \cite{uvg}, at
$3840\times2160$ resolution, 8-bit depth and 4:2:0 chroma subsampling, each
encoded at four quantization points (\texttt{cq-level} 20, 32, 43 and 55) over
five frames sampled uniformly along the whole clip, since in AI coding
consecutive frames are nearly identical. The extraction ran at
\texttt{cpu-used=0}, because the label is only faithful if every partition type
was evaluated: an extraction at \texttt{cpu-used=6} produced zero counts for the
six extended classes, exactly the ones the second stage must predict.

The resulting dataset contains 26.98 million logged partition-tree nodes, of
which 10.07 million are decision nodes of 16, 32 and 64 samples; the rest are
8$\times$8 leaves of constant label, left out of the modeling. It was split by
sequence into ten training, three validation and three reserved test sequences,
frozen before any measurement, and the eight evaluation sequences belong to the
CTC Class A1 set, absent from the UVG corpus, so no sequence is reused across
training, testing and evaluation, which rules out data leakage.

\subsection{Prediction Models and Training}

Both stages use multilayer perceptrons (MLPs), one per node size, trained
independently for the 16$\times$16, 32$\times$32 and 64$\times$64 levels; the
8$\times$8 level is terminal and is not modeled. The topology is common to all six
networks and deliberately shallow: two hidden layers with 64 and 32 ReLU units
and a linear output whose softmax gives three class probabilities in the first
stage and two in the second, for 27{,}759 parameters in total. They were trained
in PyTorch \cite{pytorch} with the AdamW optimizer \cite{adamw}, a learning rate
of $1 \times 10^{-3}$, a weight decay of $1 \times 10^{-4}$, batches of 4096
samples and a fixed budget of 30 epochs, under hard-label cross entropy and with
no distillation term. The features are standardized during training and the
standardization is folded into the first linear layer at export time, so the
encoder feeds raw features and the models execute natively within the encoding
pipeline; this parity was verified over 192 random vectors, with a maximum
difference of $1.35 \times 10^{-7}$. Over 1{,}816{,}393 held-out decision nodes of
the reserved sequences, the expected calibration error of the first stage is
0.0112, so reading its thresholds as confidence levels is verified rather than
assumed.

\subsection{Pruning Policies and Encoder Integration}

The first-stage pruner applies the three actions drawn in Fig.~\ref{fig_snp} on
the probabilities $p_{\mathrm{none}}$, $p_{\mathrm{split}}$ and
$p_{\mathrm{rest}}$ of the NONE, split and remaining classes: it terminates the
node as NONE and cuts the whole subtree when $p_{\mathrm{none}} >
\tau_{\mathrm{none}}$, which is the primary lever of time savings; otherwise it
skips NONE and recurses only into the four sub-blocks when $p_{\mathrm{split}} >
\tau_{\mathrm{split}}$; otherwise it disables the rectangular candidates, and the
extended ones with them by dependency, when $p_{\mathrm{rest}} <
\tau_{\mathrm{rest}}$. Two operating points were frozen before the final
campaign: the balanced one uses $\tau_{\mathrm{none}} = 0.95$,
$\tau_{\mathrm{split}} = 0.90$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{rest}} = 0.20$, the aggressive
one 0.60, 0.85 and 0.40.

The second-stage pruner marks the node to skip the extended partitions when
$p_{\mathrm{ext}}$, the estimated probability that an extended type wins the node,
falls below a threshold $\theta$ kept per node size, leaving NONE, the split and
the rectangular types untouched. The threshold is per level because the values
that discard the same fraction of true winners differ widely: calibrated on the
reserved sequences to lose a tenth of the nodes whose optimal type is extended,
the deployed values are 0.0910, 0.1031 and 0.0144 at the 16, 32 and 64-sample
levels, and a stronger calibration gives 0.1776, 0.1627 and 0.0321. Both pruners
were implemented in C inside libaom v3.10.0, each behind its own compile-time
guard whose disabled build reproduces the reference bitstream byte for byte,
which is what allows the contribution of the second to be attributed to its
decisions. The policy only removes candidates and never forces an illegal one, so
SNP-AV1 is fully compliant with the AV1 specification and does not affect the
bitstream syntax.

\section{Results and Comparisons}
\label{sec_results}

The developed solution was evaluated using the libaom v3.10.0 AV1 reference
software, following the AOM Common Test Conditions (CTC) \cite{ctc} and under the
AI encoder configuration. The eight UHD 4K sequences of the CTC Class A1 test set,
named in Table~\ref{tab_seq}, are all at $3840\times2160$ resolution, 10-bit depth
and 4:2:0 chroma subsampling \cite{xipha1}. The first 15 frames of each were
encoded, as the CTC specifies for the AI scenario, at \texttt{cq-level} 20, 32, 43
and 55, against the unmodified libaom at \texttt{cpu-used=0} as anchor. The BD-BR
\cite{bjontegaard} is computed over the luma peak signal-to-noise ratio, and
every value reported here is a coding efficiency loss. Writing
$t_{\mathrm{SNP}}(s,q)$ and $t_{\mathrm{anc}}(s,q)$ for the encoding times of the
evaluated SNP-AV1 configuration and of the anchor on sequence $s$ at quality
point $q$, over the sequence set $S$ and the quality set $Q$,

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{TS} = \frac{1}{|S|} \sum_{s \in S} \frac{1}{|Q|} \sum_{q \in Q}
\left( 1 - \frac{t_{\mathrm{SNP}}(s,q)}{t_{\mathrm{anc}}(s,q)} \right),
\label{eq_ts}
\end{equation}

reported as a percentage. All BD-BR and TS results come from this grid; the cost
shares of Section~\ref{sec_shapes} were measured on the UVG corpus instead. Every
configuration was encoded once; repeating the anchor and the balanced first stage
five times on Crosswalk gives a standard deviation of 0.23 percentage points on
TS, so differences below twice that value are not resolved, while the BD-BR
values are exact. No native speed preset is placed beside SNP-AV1: a preset
reconfigures every coding stage, so a side-by-side reading would rank
arrangements of different scope rather than partition-search techniques.
Fig.~\ref{fig_space} places the six measured operating points against the anchor,
and moving among them is a matter of thresholds alone, without retraining or
recompiling.

The second stage is measured marginally, because it acts only on the residue of
nodes the first stage did not commit. Over the balanced base it contributes 1.02
percentage points of time savings, about 4.4 times the measured standard
deviation, for 0.018 percentage points of BD-BR. That is a price of 0.018 point
of coding efficiency per point of time bought, against the 0.061 charged by the
only alternative the deployed solution leaves to its user, which is loosening the
thresholds of the first stage: the second pruner is about 3.4 times cheaper, and
Fig.~\ref{fig_space}(a) shows the two prices as the two slopes that leave the
same operating point. Its stronger calibration costs 0.040, still below the price
of the knob, which is why the milder one is deployed. Sequence by sequence, at
time savings matched by interpolating the first-stage thresholds, the stacked
point wins in six of the eight sequences, two of them by strict Pareto dominance,
and loses only where the extended axis is barely exercised.

This additivity is a property of the operating point, and not of the pruner. Over
the aggressive base the same second stage adds 0.17 percentage points, below the
resolution of the arrangement and above it in one sequence instead of six: with
the NONE threshold loosened to 0.60, the first stage commits the nodes so early
that they seldom reach the point at which the extended partitions would be
decided, and the two pruners fall back to disputing the same residue. That is the
whole width of Fig.~\ref{fig_space}(b), whose three points cover 0.65 percentage
points of time savings.

Table~\ref{tab_seq} opens the two deployed points sequence by sequence, and what
it exposes is content dispersion, not a swept range. At the balanced calibration
the time savings average 18.74\% $\pm$ 3.40 percentage points, from 13.78\% on
NocturneDance to 22.89\% on Tango, and the BD-BR averages 0.586\% $\pm$ 0.278,
from 0.168\% to 1.139\% on those same two sequences. The aggressive calibration
keeps that shape and lifts the level, to 31.68\% $\pm$ 4.17 of time savings, from
26.03\% to 38.80\%, at 1.409\% $\pm$ 0.377 of BD-BR, from 0.934\% to 2.206\%. The
dispersion is content and not measurement, since it is more than ten times the
standard deviation of the repeated encodings.

Read as time bought per point of coding efficiency ceded, the eight sequences
have a median of 35 points of time savings per point of BD-BR at the balanced
calibration and of 22 at the aggressive one, the rising price the aggregate
showed. The two columns are the two ends of one mechanism, and they correlate by
rank at 0.79 ($p = 0.02$) at the balanced point: the time a sequence yields is
bounded by how often the search itself settles for the unpartitioned node, and
the efficiency it cedes by how often the first stage commits where that search
would not have. The coupling dissolves at the aggressive point, at 0.31
($p = 0.46$), which may be attributed to a threshold loose enough to commit nodes
almost everywhere. The overhead is reported rather than omitted: feature
extraction and inference together stay below one third of one percent of the
encoding time, so the savings come from the pruning decisions and not from the
lightness of each inference.

\section{Conclusions}

This paper presented SNP-AV1, a machine-learning solution that stacks two neural
pruners at two distinct stages of the AV1 intra-frame partition search, reaching
an average time savings of 18.74\% with a BD-BR of 0.59\%, and 32.16\% with
1.42\% under a stronger calibration. The first pruner reads thirty-six pre-search
features and decides how much of the subtree is opened before any
rate-distortion cost is paid; the second adds the measured RD cost of NONE and
decides whether the extended partitions, one third of the local search time, are
worth evaluating. Both were implemented in C inside libaom v3.10.0 and evaluated
on the CTC Class A1 UHD 4K sequences under the AI configuration. Measured marginally
over the balanced base, the second pruner buys time about 3.4 times cheaper than
the thresholds of the first stage and wins in six of the eight sequences; the
advantage collapses over the aggressive base, where the first stage commits the
nodes before they reach the second, which is the operating-point limit of the
stacking.
