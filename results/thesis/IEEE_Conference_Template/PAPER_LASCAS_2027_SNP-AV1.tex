\begin{abstract}
The AOMedia Video 1 (AV1) is an open and royalty-free video codec whose
intra-frame coding relies on a recursive partition search evaluating ten
partition shapes per node. This search is exhaustive by construction and
dominates the intra-frame encoding time at the highest quality configuration, a
burden that grows with 4K content. This paper presents the Stacked Neural Pruners
for AV1 (SNP-AV1), a machine-learning solution for the AV1 intra-frame partition
search applying two neural networks at two distinct points of the search. The
first reads thirty-six attributes of low-cost causal context and decides, before
any rate-distortion evaluation is paid, whether the node is committed to the
undivided partition, whether the search recurses only on the quad-tree split, or
whether the rectangular candidates are discarded. The second adds the measured
rate, distortion and rate-distortion cost of the undivided candidate, and decides
whether the four AB and the two 4-way shapes are evaluated at all. Experimental
results show that SNP-AV1 achieves an average time savings of 18.74\%, with a
BD-BR of 0.59\%, reaching 32.16\% and 1.42\% under a stronger calibration.
SNP-AV1 does not dominate the native speed presets, which reconfigure the whole
encoder, at a cost of a higher BD-BR, but it delivers operating points the preset
ladder leaves uncovered.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec_intro}

Video is the dominant workload of the Internet, and the share of users watching
online video every month has kept growing over the last years \cite{statista}.
Alongside the standards defined by the international standardization bodies, such
as the High Efficiency Video Coding (HEVC) \cite{hevc} and the Versatile Video
Coding (VVC) \cite{vvc} produced by the Motion Picture Expert Group (ISO/IEC
MPEG) and by the Video Coding Experts Group (ITU-T VCEG), the Alliance for Open
Media (AOMedia) maintains the AOMedia Video 1 (AV1) \cite{av1site} as an open and
royalty-free alternative.

AV1 follows the state-of-the-art flow of hybrid video encoders, splitting the
process into intra-frame prediction, inter-frame prediction, transforms,
quantization, in-loop filters and entropy coding, and introduced new techniques
in each one of these steps \cite{han2021,chen2018}. This coding efficiency comes
at the price of computational effort: AV1 was shown to be superior to HEVC under
certain conditions \cite{grois2016}, but encoding videos of the same bitrate and
resolution took twice as long \cite{layek2017}, a problem aggravated in 4K
content.

One of the largest individual costs of AV1 intra-frame coding is the block
partitioning decision. Every superblock is submitted to a recursive search that,
at each node of the partition tree, evaluates the rate-distortion (RD) cost
\cite{sullivan1998} of ten candidate shapes and keeps the cheapest one,
descending recursively whenever the quad-tree split wins. It is exhaustive by
construction, since the encoder only knows which shape is best after actually
coding the block under each of them, and in the reference software libaom
\cite{libaom} this regime is what the highest quality preset,
\texttt{cpu-used=0}, delivers.

There are a few works in the literature targeting the computational effort of
AV1, and none of them attacks this search. The works \cite{xu2020,xu2021,%
jeong2019} act on the intra prediction mode decision loop by heuristic criteria,
so they target a different tool, which prevents a direct and fair comparison. The
works \cite{correa2020,netto2022} are hardware solutions for the AV1 intra
prediction, so their target is the circuit and not the encoder search, and
\cite{kim2019} applies decision trees to AV1 inter-prediction. Learning-based
partitioning solutions do exist for VVC \cite{saldanha2022,he2021,chen2019}, and
their concept can all be adapted to work with the AV1 coding tools, but they were
designed for the quad-tree with nested multi-type tree and evaluated on another
encoder, so a direct and fair comparison is not possible. None of these works
explore learned pruning of the AV1 intra-frame partition search itself.

This paper presents SNP-AV1, which is not another speed preset but a learned
layer for encoding time reduction confined to the intra-frame partition search,
replacing specific parts of the exhaustive exploration by neural decisions at two
complementary moments of the rate-distortion optimization and leaving every other
encoding stage untouched. The main strategy is to remove candidates progressively as
stronger evidence becomes available: the first stage
decides how much of the tree deserves to be opened at all, from causal context
whose extraction cost is negligible, and the second whether the most expensive
family of candidates is worth evaluating, from the rate-distortion evidence the
encoder has by then measured. Over the eight 4K sequences of the Common Test
Conditions (CTC) Class A1, SNP-AV1 reaches an average time savings of 18.74\%
with a Bj{\o}ntegaard delta bitrate (BD-BR) of 0.586\%.

\section{Block Partitioning in AV1}

The basic unit of the AV1 partitioning is the superblock, which has 128$\times$128
or 64$\times$64 pixels, and the structure that subdivides it is called partition
tree \cite{han2021}. AV1 allows ten partition types at each node \cite{chen2018},
which Fig. \ref{fig_shapes} organizes into the four families used throughout this
paper: the two structural decisions, PARTITION\_NONE, hereafter NONE, which
codes the node undivided, and PARTITION\_SPLIT, the quad-tree split into four
square sub-blocks;
the two rectangular shapes, PARTITION\_HORZ and PARTITION\_VERT; the four
asymmetric AB shapes, HORZ\_A, HORZ\_B, VERT\_A and VERT\_B, combining one
rectangular half with two square sub-blocks; and the two 4-way shapes, HORZ\_4
and VERT\_4, of 4:1 aspect ratio. The AB and the 4-way families are jointly
called the extended partitions.

The evaluation order inside a node is fixed and defines which information has
already been paid at each point of the flow: NONE first, then the
quad-tree split, then the rectangular shapes, and finally the extended partitions. A
pruner acting before the first evaluation is blind to any rate-distortion number,
and one acting after NONE has the measured rate, distortion and RD
cost of the undivided candidate, at the price of never saving the cost of
producing them.

The cost distribution in Fig. \ref{fig_shapes} was measured with the native
partition timing instrumentation of libaom over 875{,}317 decision nodes of the
4K dataset described in Section \ref{sec_data}, at \texttt{cpu-used=0}, taking as
local work the sum of the nine non-recursive candidates of a node. The main
conclusion when observing it is that the cost splits into three blocks of
comparable size, and that the six extended shapes are one of them, consuming
34.3\% of the local search time against 30.1\% of NONE and 35.6\% of
the rectangular shapes. It is important to emphasize that no single extended
shape exceeds 7.22\% of that time, which explains why this cost went unnoticed in
analyses examining candidates in isolation. It also concentrates in the large
nodes, reaching 50.0\% at 32$\times$32 and 51.0\% at 64$\times$64 pixels, which
are the nodes a 4K frame produces in abundance.

This work is focused on the recursive block partitioning decision of the AV1
intra-frame coding. The remaining decisions taken inside a coding block are not
included in the partition search loop attacked here, such as the intra prediction
modes, the recursive-based filtering, the chroma-from-luma prediction, the intra
block copy and the transform type search, and therefore they are not focused on
this work. The inter-frame partition search is also outside
the scope, since the solution is trained and evaluated exclusively under the
All-Intra (AI) encoder configuration.

\section{Proposed SNP-AV1 Solution}
\label{sec_method}

The Stacked Neural Pruners for AV1 (SNP-AV1) is a fast decision algorithm
composed of two neural pruners inserted at two distinct stages of the search of a
node, as shown in Fig. \ref{fig_snp}, removing candidates progressively as
stronger evidence becomes available.

The two stages are not two levels of sophistication of the same idea. The first
acts before any evaluation, so its reach is maximal, since a correct commitment
removes the whole subtree, including the evaluation of NONE itself,
which is the only cost no later pruner can save; in exchange, it decides blind to
rate-distortion. The second acts afterwards, so its information is richer, at the
price of a structurally limited reach. This is what makes them complementary
rather than competing: two pruners add up to the extent that the candidates they
remove are disjoint, and the second targets the extended family, which the first
never aims at.

\subsection{Attribute Design}

The attribute vector of the first stage has thirty-six entries in three blocks.
Twenty-four are luma descriptors of the node and of its hierarchical context,
covering variance overall and per quadrant, gradient energy and orientation, row
and column profiles, edge strength and density, and the contrast against the
2n$\times$2n parent and the three sibling sub-blocks. Eight carry the causal
partitioning neighborhood: availability of the above and left neighbors, their
decided widths and heights in log2 units, and the granularity and anisotropy of
that neighborhood. The last four carry the effective dequantization step of the
luma DC coefficient, the normalized position in the frame and the node depth.

The criterion for inclusion was the cost of obtaining each attribute where the
pruner is consulted: the twelve neighborhood, quantization and position
attributes are a free read of state the native search already maintains, and the
luma descriptors require only arithmetic linear in the area of the node, so all
selected attributes are natively available during encoding, ensuring zero
computational overhead for attribute extraction. The second stage reads these
thirty-six plus three that exist only afterwards, the logarithm of the rate, of
the distortion and of the RD cost measured for NONE, which is why it
is better informed and can never act earlier.

\subsection{Dataset Construction}
\label{sec_data}

The reference software libaom v3.10.0 was instrumented under a compile-time guard
to record, at every node of the intra-frame partition search, the ground-truth
decision taken by the encoder itself, together with the luma samples, the causal
context, the quantization state, the position and the rate-distortion result of
NONE. The guard is necessary because a binary that writes to disk at
every node cannot be the one that measures wall-clock acceleration; with it
disabled, the build is verified byte-identical to the untouched reference tree.

The dataset was built from the sixteen 4K sequences of the UVG dataset
\cite{uvg}, at 3840$\times$2160, 8-bit depth and 4:2:0 chroma subsampling, each
encoded at four constant quality (CQ) values, 20, 32, 43 and 55, over five frames
sampled uniformly along the whole clip, since in AI coding consecutive frames are
nearly identical. The extraction ran entirely at \texttt{cpu-used=0}, because the
label is only faithful if it comes from a search that actually evaluated every
candidate shape, whereas any faster preset already contains heuristic pruning and
would contaminate it. This was verified rather than assumed: an extraction at
\texttt{cpu-used=6} produced zero counts for the six extended classes, which are
exactly the ones the second stage must predict.

The resulting dataset contains 26.98 million samples, of which 10.07 million are
decision nodes of 16, 32 and 64 pixels; the rest are 8$\times$8 leaves whose
label is constant and which are left out of the modeling. It was partitioned by
sequence into ten training, three validation and three reserved test sequences,
frozen before any measurement, and the eight sequences used for the final
evaluation belong to the CTC Class A1 set, none of which appears in the UVG
corpus. Consequently, no video sequence was reused across the training, testing,
or evaluation datasets, thus avoiding data leakage and overfitting.

\subsection{Network Architecture and Training}

Both stages use multilayer perceptrons, one per node size, trained independently
for the 16$\times$16, 32$\times$32 and 64$\times$64 levels; the 8$\times$8 level
is terminal and is not modeled. The topology is common to all variants and
deliberately shallow: two hidden layers of 64 and 32 units with rectified
activation, followed by a linear output layer. The first stage takes thirty-six
inputs and emits three classes, whose softmax is the probability of the node
being undivided, being split, or taking any of the remaining shapes; the second
takes thirty-nine inputs and emits two, evaluating or skipping the extended
partitions. This gives 4{,}547 parameters per level in the first stage and
4{,}706 in the second, for 27{,}759 across the six deployed networks.

The networks were trained in PyTorch \cite{pytorch} with the AdamW optimizer
\cite{adamw}, a learning rate of $1 \times 10^{-3}$, a weight decay of
$1 \times 10^{-4}$, batches of 4096 samples and a fixed budget of 30 epochs
without early stopping, under hard-label cross entropy without class weighting.
The attributes are standardized during training and the standardization is folded
into the first linear layer at export time, so the encoder feeds raw attributes
and the weight layout matches what the native inference routine expects, which
dispenses with any new inference code in C and ensures that the models execute
natively within the encoding pipeline; this parity was verified over 192 random
vectors, with a maximum difference of $1.35 \times 10^{-7}$.

\subsection{Pruning Policies and Encoder Integration}

The first-stage pruner applies three actions in cascade. If the probability of
the undivided class exceeds $\tau_{\mathrm{none}}$, all splits are disabled and
the node is decided as NONE, which cuts the whole subtree without
evaluating any candidate; this is the primary lever of time savings. Otherwise,
if the probability of the split class exceeds $\tau_{\mathrm{split}}$, the shape
candidates are skipped and the search recurses only into the four sub-blocks.
Otherwise, if the probability of the remaining class falls below
$\tau_{\mathrm{rest}}$, the rectangular candidates are disabled, which by
dependency also disables the extended shapes. Otherwise the node proceeds to the
full search. Two operating points were frozen before the final campaign: the
balanced one uses $\tau_{\mathrm{none}} = 0.95$, $\tau_{\mathrm{split}} = 0.90$
and $\tau_{\mathrm{rest}} = 0.20$, and the aggressive one uses 0.60, 0.85
and 0.40.

The second-stage pruner marks the node to skip the extended partitions when the
estimated probability falls below a threshold kept per node size, leaving
NONE, the quad-tree split and the rectangular shapes untouched. The
per-level choice is experimental, since the values that discard the same fraction
of true winners differ widely: the deployed calibration uses 0.0910, 0.1031 and
0.0144 at the 16, 32 and 64-pixel levels, a stronger one 0.1776, 0.1627
and 0.0321.

Both pruners were implemented in C inside libaom v3.10.0. The policy only removes
candidates and never forces an illegal one, so the search flow remains valid,
SNP-AV1 is fully compliant with the AV1 specification and does not affect the
bitstream syntax, and the generated bitstream is decodable by any conforming AV1
decoder. The control surface is inert by default, verified by re-encoding a
reference point with the second stage switched off and obtaining a bitstream
byte-identical to the first-stage baseline. It is important to highlight the
structural limits of the proposal: the first stage is blind to rate-distortion
evidence by construction, and the second can never recover the cost of the
undivided candidate it depends upon, which caps its reach however accurate it
becomes.

\section{Results and Comparisons}
\label{sec_results}

The developed solution was evaluated using the libaom v3.10.0 AV1 reference
software, following the AOM Common Test Conditions (CTC) \cite{ctc} and under the
AI encoder configuration. The eight 4K sequences of the CTC Class A1 test set are
BoxingPractice, Crosswalk, FoodMarket2, Neon1224, NocturneDance, PierSeaSide,
Tango and TimeLapse, all at 3840$\times$2160, 10 bits and 4:2:0
\cite{xipha1}. The first 15 frames of each were encoded, as
the CTC specifies for the AI scenario, at CQ 20, 32, 43 and 55, against the
unmodified libaom at \texttt{cpu-used=0} as anchor. The BD-BR \cite{bjontegaard}
is computed over the luma peak signal-to-noise ratio; a negative BD-BR denotes a
coding efficiency gain, so all values here, being positive, are losses. The time
savings (TS) express the wall-clock time saved relative to the anchor. Writing
$t_{\mathrm{cfg}}(s,q)$ and $t_{\mathrm{anc}}(s,q)$ for the encoding times of the
evaluated configuration and of the anchor on sequence $s$ at quality point $q$,
over the sequence set $S$ and the quality set $Q$,

\begin{equation}
\mathrm{TS} = \frac{1}{|S|} \sum_{s \in S} \frac{1}{|Q|} \sum_{q \in Q}
\left( 1 - \frac{t_{\mathrm{cfg}}(s,q)}{t_{\mathrm{anc}}(s,q)} \right),
\label{eq_ts}
\end{equation}

reported as a percentage. The ratio is taken per quality point before any
average, so the four points weigh equally and the result is not dominated by the
most expensive one. All BD-BR and TS results come from this grid; the figures of
Section \ref{sec_method} are distribution and classification statistics of the
UVG dataset.

The preset rows of Table \ref{tab_ctc} must be qualified before any number is
read, because the two arms do not have the same scope: a preset is a global
switch, and moving from \texttt{cpu-used=0} to 1 enables dozens of heuristics
across the whole encoder, of which the partition search is only one, whereas
SNP-AV1 acts inside that single stage. The preset column therefore answers
whether the speed knob already suffices, and does not rank partition-search
techniques. Under that reading, \texttt{cpu-used=1} delivers more time savings
than the
balanced deployed point, 32.59\% against 18.74\%, at a lower BD-BR of 0.449\%
against 0.586\%, and it also dominates the aggressive point, so no operating
point of SNP-AV1 dominates the ladder on both axes. What the ladder does not
offer is granularity, and Fig. \ref{fig_space} is where that reading becomes
visible: the preset curve jumps from \texttt{cpu-used=0} straight to roughly
32.6\%, and the six measured points of SNP-AV1 populate that void, three of them
between 17.72\% and 19.81\%, obtained by threshold alone, without retraining or
recompiling.

The overhead is reported rather than omitted: the complete path of SNP-AV1,
attribute extraction and inference together, and that of the native learned
pruner both stay below one third of one percent of the encoding time, so the time
savings come from the pruning decisions and not from the lightness of each
inference. It is worth noting, however, that this operating region is reached
from a modest budget: six deliberately shallow networks of 27{,}759 parameters in
total, a fixed schedule of 30 epochs, and a model selection limited to a few
ablations run on the validation split and never on the test sequences. Both the
architecture and the training schedule were therefore barely explored, which is
itself the opportunity, since the margin available from a larger model, a longer
schedule or a wider training corpus remains untouched and is a concrete direction
for future work. Finally, no quantitative comparison with the AV1 works of Section
\ref{sec_intro} is offered, because none of them acts on the partition search:
they target the intra prediction mode decision or the circuit, under different
libaom versions and sequence sets, so their numbers measure a different tool and
would not be comparable with the ones reported here.

\section{Conclusions}

This paper presented SNP-AV1, a machine-learning solution that stacks two neural
pruners at two distinct stages of the AV1 intra-frame partition search, reaching
an average time savings of 18.74\% with a BD-BR of 0.59\%, and 32.16\% with
1.42\% under a stronger calibration. The first pruner decides how much of the
subtree is opened at all before any rate-distortion cost is paid, and the second
adds the measured cost of the undivided candidate to decide whether the extended
partitions are evaluated; because the two candidate sets are disjoint at the
deployed point, the second adds 1.02 percentage points of time savings for 0.018
of BD-BR, at a total overhead below 0.32\%. No operating point
dominates the native preset ladder on both axes, since \texttt{cpu-used=1}
delivers 32.59\% at a BD-BR of 0.449\%, although that preset reconfigures the
whole encoder while SNP-AV1 acts only inside the partition search, where it
delivers encoding time reduction at a granularity the ladder does not offer.
